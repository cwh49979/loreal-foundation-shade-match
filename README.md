# loreal foundation: how to choose between True Match and Infallible, match your shade, and pay under retail

Searching "loreal foundation" usually means you've hit one of two walls. Either there are six bottles in front of you and no obvious difference between them, or you already know the one you want and just don't want to pay full price for it. Both problems have answers, and neither requires a beauty counter.

L'Oréal Paris runs roughly two foundation families plus a couple of strays, and the formulas behind them do genuinely different jobs. The price side is messier than most people expect: the same bottle can swing from about US$15 to over €27 depending on which channel you buy from. Here's how to work out which one you want, then how to knock something back off the receipt.

## Two families, and almost everything else is a variation

True Match and Infallible cover the bulk of what's on shelf.

**True Match** is the skin-like line. The Super-Blendable foundation is buildable rather than heavy, and the shade system is the whole point of the name. Brand listings describe the formula as built around shade-matching pigments and a creamy, gliding texture rather than maximum coverage.

**Infallible** is the wear-time line. The Fresh Wear 32H liquid was described in European listings as high coverage with a lightweight feel, and the bottle moved to a 100% recycled format with that update. The version sold in the US is labelled "Infallible Fresh Wear 32 Hr Liquid Foundation"; some EU listings mention SPF 25 and vitamin C in the formula description. Formula details do vary by market, so read the carton in front of you rather than a review written in another country.

Then there's the format split: liquid, powder, and tinted serum. The powder version under the Infallible name is a one-step powder foundation that the brand describes as matte-finishing, water-, sweat- and heat-resistant, with up to 24 hours of wear.

## Matching your shade without testing it on your jaw

True Match shade names follow a letter-and-number pattern: the letter signals undertone, the number signals depth. W for warm, N for neutral, C for cool, with the number climbing as the shade gets deeper. If you've worn a shade that looked slightly grey on you, you were probably on the wrong letter rather than the wrong number.

A few things that make online shade-picking less of a gamble:

- Shop in daylight, near a window, and photograph your bare jawline and neck together. Artificial bathroom lighting shifts everything warm.
- Read reviews from people with your undertone, not just your depth. "Too orange" and "too grey" are undertone complaints pretending to be depth complaints.
- Tinted serums forgive more than full coverage. If you're between shades, that's the format that won't punish you for being half a step off.
- Buy from a retailer with a decent returns policy on opened cosmetics, because a wrong shade is the most common reason a foundation order goes back.

## What actually decides the price

The formula costs what it costs. The channel is where the money moves.

Mass retailers run the deepest baseline prices — the Infallible 32H liquid showing at US$14.97 and the powder at US$18.99 are typical of that tier. Airport duty-free sat at €27.40 for a comparable product, and a Polish duty-free listing only came down to PLN 62 once a 20% discount was applied. Pharmacy chains and department stores sit somewhere in between, and tend to be where shade ranges are widest in markets like Singapore, Malaysia and Australia.

None of that is stable. Sale windows move prices more than any single retailer does, which is why the useful habit isn't chasing the cheapest store — it's stacking a cashback platform on top of whatever sale is already running.

## Why the rebate sometimes never arrives

Most failed cashback isn't a platform problem. It's a browser problem.

- **Ad blockers and VPNs** interfere with affiliate tracking. Turn them off for the purchase.
- **Other tabs and other links** can overwrite the tracking cookie. ShopBack's own guidance is that its link needs to be the last one you used before landing on the retailer.
- **Coupon codes the platform doesn't list** can void the order at some retailers. Sephora's terms are explicit about this.
- **App purchases are excluded** at some stores. Sephora only tracks website orders, and requires you to clear your cart before clicking through from ShopBack.
- **Gift cards, vouchers, taxes and shipping** don't earn cashback at all. Prorated only.
- **Returns and cancellations** wipe the cashback out entirely. Partial returns get adjusted.

Beauty retailers also confirm slowly because their return windows are long. The Sephora Australia page quoted 14 days to track and 105 days to confirm; the New Zealand page said 2 days to track and 65 days to confirm. Either way, this is not money you'll have before you need a replacement bottle.

## Questions that come up before the first order

**Does cashback apply if the foundation is already on sale?**
Generally yes. Cashback is calculated on your order amount, with taxes, shipping and fees excluded. Sale prices don't usually kill it — unlisted coupon codes sometimes do.

**Can I stack it with my loyalty points?**
Usually, yes. Loyalty points and cashback are paid from different pockets. The exception is a "new customer" discount stacked on your very first order, which can conflict with referral bonus conditions.

**How much do I need before I can withdraw?**
Each market sets its own threshold — Australia's referral conditions reference A$20, the global support page references $5, Taiwan references NT$200. Check your account page rather than a blog post, including this one.

**Is it worth it for one bottle?**
On a US$15 foundation, a 4% rebate is 60 cents. That's not worth installing anything for. It becomes worth it when you're restocking foundation, concealer, mascara and a serum in the same order, or when a pharmacy chain runs an upsized campaign on the L'Oréal range. Treat it as a habit, not a one-off hack.

**Which L'Oréal foundation should a first-timer buy?**
If you want forgiving coverage you can build up, True Match Super-Blendable. If you need it to survive a commute, humidity and a long day, the Infallible Fresh Wear liquid. If your skin is oily and you hate the feel of liquid, the Infallible powder. If you want barely-there coverage, the True Match tinted serum. Everything else on the shelf is a variation on those four decisions.

## The short version

Pick your format by how your skin behaves, pick your shade by undertone letter first and depth number second, and buy from whichever channel is having a sale.
